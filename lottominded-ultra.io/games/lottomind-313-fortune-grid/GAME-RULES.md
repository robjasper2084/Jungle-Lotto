# LottoMind 313: Fortune Grid — Game Rules

Fortune Grid is an original, entertainment-only strategy game. Players establish fictional ventures and partnerships in represented Detroit districts; they do not buy neighborhoods. It does not predict lottery outcomes.

## Match setup

- Solo: one local player plus 1–3 CPU players.
- Local pass-and-play: 2–4 local players, with remaining seats optionally CPU-controlled.
- Quick 313 and Daily: three rounds. Standard: thirteen rounds.

Each player begins at the LottoMind 313 Hub with 1,313 Detroit Dollars, 3 Focus Tokens, and no ventures. Detroit Dollars exist only inside the current match and never become LottoMind Credits.

## Turn sequence

1. Roll a Movement Cube (1–6) and Signal Orb (0–9).
2. Choose a legal branch when routes split.
3. Move node by node and resolve the destination.
4. Add the Signal digit to the current player's four-digit strip.
5. Launch or upgrade a venture, use a special system, manage a portfolio, then end the turn.

The seeded match generator determines both results and card order. Saved games include the seed, ruleset version, and action log.

## Ventures

Available ventures may be launched, then developed through Pop-Up, Local Favorite, Creative Hub, and City Landmark. Landing at another player's venture pays a Collaboration Fee. Operating all three ventures in one network activates its unique bonus. Portfolio actions include launch, upgrade, sponsor, temporary partnership, trade, sell-back, and view.

## Recovery and victory

A player with no Detroit Dollars enters Rebuild Mode: new launches pause, but grants, partnerships, and sell-back remain available. The player returns after rebuilding at least 180 Detroit Dollars.

Final score includes Legacy Points, completed networks, venture development, Signal achievements, remaining Focus, and community standing. The results screen shows every component.

## Signals

Digits may be sorted, reordered, copied, saved locally, or sent to the existing Saved Numbers Wallet when authenticated and configured. Replacing a digit costs Focus. All draws and sequences are simulated, in-game, and entertainment-only.
